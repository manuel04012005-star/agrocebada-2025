Barley yield forecast

We trained machine learning and deep learning models on 138 barley parcels in Hidalgo, Puebla and Tlaxcala to estimate the 2025 yield (t/ha) of 59 blind parcels, using Sentinel-2 and Planet satellite indices, CHIRPS/CHIRTS climate data and INEGI topography.

Key finding: the municipality average (RMSE 0.50) beats every model. Location explains most of the yield; satellite, climate and terrain features add little extra information with only 138 parcels and partly rounded yields (2.5, 3.0, 3.5 t/ha).

Which variables relate to yield? (Spearman correlation)

Longitude (location) dominates. Parcels that green up early (June) and stay green until October–November yield more; more rain and higher maximum temperature relate to lower yield.

Method

  • Data cleaning: satellite images with more than 20% cloud cover removed; Sentinel-2 and Planet kept as separate features.
  • Features (48): monthly NDVI, NDWI and MSI (Jun–Nov 2025), season peak, mean and peak date; rainfall and temperature (Apr–Oct 2025); elevation, slope and coordinates.
  • Feature selection: top 15 features by Spearman correlation, chosen inside each cross-validation fold.
  • Models: Ridge regression, Random Forest, Extra Trees and a neural network (MLP, Keras).
  • Limitations: small sample (138 parcels), coarse climate data (~5 km), rounded yields in some municipalities, and predictions that shrink toward each state's average.
